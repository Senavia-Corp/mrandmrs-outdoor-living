A residential pool and a commercial pool in Florida are built with the same materials by the same kind of crew, and from there the paths split. A residential pool is governed by the Florida Building Code's residential swimming pool chapter and the Residential Swimming Pool Safety Act, permitted and inspected by the local building department, and designed to the owner's taste. A commercial pool is also a public pool under the Florida Department of Health's rule, which adds a second permit, a prescribed set of engineering requirements, and obligations that continue after the pool opens.

This guide lays the two side by side for owners, boards and developers deciding which project they actually have.

## Which rules apply

| | Residential pool | Commercial (public) pool |
|---|---|---|
| Primary rule | Florida Building Code, residential swimming pool chapter | Florida Building Code plus Florida Administrative Code Chapter 64E-9 (public pools) |
| Safety law | Residential Swimming Pool Safety Act (barrier or equivalent) | Public pool rule: barriers, signage, lifesaving equipment, suction-outlet protection |
| Accessibility | Not required | Federal accessibility standards apply to public accommodations |
| Who decides design details | The owner, within the code | The rule prescribes hydraulics, treatment, markings, decks, lighting and more |

The classification is not a choice. If the pool serves more than one household, it is almost always public, and the Department of Health makes the determination. Our guide to [commercial pool construction for decision-makers](/blogs/commercial-pool-construction-in-florida-what-decision-makers-must-know) covers the classification in more detail.

## Who permits and inspects

| | Residential | Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Building permit | Local building department | Local building department |
| Health permit | None | Department of Health construction permit before building, operating permit before opening |
| Inspections | Building department, at construction stages | Building department at stages, plus Department of Health before opening and periodically after |
| Plans | Engineer-sealed structural plans | Engineer-sealed plans drawn to the public pool rule, reviewed by both agencies |

For the residential side, [which permits a Florida pool needs](/blogs/what-permits-are-required-for-pool-construction-in-florida) walks through the package and the inspection sequence.

## What the design has to include

A residential pool is designed around how a family will use it: the shape, the spa, the sun shelf, the finish, the lighting, the features. The code sets the floor, mainly on structure, electrical, suction outlets and the barrier, and the owner chooses everything above it.

A public pool is designed to a rule that specifies the turnover rate and filtration, the disinfection and pH control, the depth profile and markings, deck widths and drainage, lighting, ladders, handrails and lifelines, the equipment room, chemical storage, signage, and an accessible means of entry. Aesthetics are layered on top of that; they do not replace it.

## Construction and equipment

The shell of a gunite public pool is built the same way as a residential one, engineered for its size and the ground it sits in. The differences are in what is attached to it: larger filtration sized to the required turnover, automated chemical control that most operators need to stay within the rule, flow meters, a dedicated equipment room, deck drainage that returns nothing to the pool, and lifesaving equipment and signage at opening. Suction-outlet protection is required on both, but a public pool's design is reviewed for it explicitly.

## Cost and schedule

A public pool of a given size costs more than a residential pool of the same size, and takes longer, because the rule prescribes more, the engineering is heavier, and two agencies review and inspect. We do not publish per-square-foot comparisons because they misrepresent both: on a residential pool the price is set by design choices the owner makes, and on a public pool by the treatment system, the deck program and accessibility more than by the water. A realistic number for either comes from a design, not a formula. For residential pools, our [pool cost estimator](/pool-cost-estimator) prices the design choices; for commercial projects, we price from a program and an engineered concept.

## Operation after opening

A residential pool is the owner's to run. A public pool is operated under the Department's rule, with records, inspections and responsibilities that continue for the life of the pool. The operator should be in the room during design, because equipment choices decide how hard that job is.

## Licensing

Florida's pool contractor licenses distinguish scope. A contractor licensed for commercial pool work can build residential pools as well; the reverse is not automatic. Before hiring for a community or hotel pool, check the license category on the DBPR site, not only that the number exists. We build both under our state licenses, which are published on every page of this site.

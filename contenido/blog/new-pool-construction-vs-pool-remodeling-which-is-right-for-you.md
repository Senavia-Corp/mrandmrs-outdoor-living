If you already own a pool, the real question is narrower than "new or remodel." It is whether the shell, the location and the layout you have can carry what you want from the backyard. If they can, a complete remodel gets you a pool that looks and runs like new for less than building new, with less disruption. If they cannot, no amount of remodeling fixes a pool that is in the wrong place or structurally finished.

Five questions decide it. Most Florida homeowners who ask us land on a complete remodel.

## Question 1: Is the shell sound?

Everything in a remodel rides on the structure. We check the shell and the bond beam for cracks and movement, look at the tile line and the coping for displacement, pressure-test the plumbing, and where there are signs of leaking or settling, look closer at the structure before pricing anything. A sound shell with an ugly surface is the ideal remodel candidate. A shell that has failed is not a remodel; it is a rebuild decision, which we cover separately in [remodel or rebuild](/blogs/remodel-or-rebuild-your-pool).

## Question 2: Is the pool where you want it?

A remodel cannot move the pool. If the pool blocks the view, sits where the outdoor kitchen should be, or is too close to the house to allow the deck you want, the layout is the problem and new construction is the only route to a different one. If the location works and the complaint is how the pool looks and performs, that is a remodel.

## Question 3: What do you actually want to change?

Write the list, then sort it into two columns.

**Remodel can do this:** interior finish, waterline tile, coping, deck material and extent, equipment (pump, filter, heater, chlorination, automation), lighting, a spa added or rebuilt, a sun shelf cut into the shallow end, steps and benches, water features, a screen enclosure, and a code-compliant barrier.

**Only new construction does this:** a different footprint or a substantially larger pool, a different depth profile throughout, a pool in a different place, or a pool and spa designed as one new structure.

If your whole list sits in the first column, the answer is a remodel. Our guide to [what to upgrade during a complete pool remodel](/blogs/what-to-upgrade-during-a-complete-pool-remodel) explains how to sequence it so you only open the pool once.

## Question 4: How much of the backyard is changing?

A pool is rarely remodeled alone. If you are also adding a pergola, an outdoor kitchen or a screen enclosure, the remodel is the moment to run the gas, electrical and plumbing those need, because the deck is already open. If you are rebuilding the entire backyard around a new layout, that is the case where new construction can make more sense, because the pool is designed with everything else rather than worked around.

## Question 5: What does each path cost on your pool?

Prices are specific to the pool, so we do not quote generic ranges. What is consistent is the structure of the comparison:

| | Complete remodel | New construction |
|---|---|---|
| Shell and excavation | Already exist | Priced from scratch, including rock or water-table work |
| Deck | Resurfaced or replaced; extent can grow | Designed and built new |
| Equipment and electrical | Replaced and brought to code | New, sized to the design |
| Finish, tile, coping | New | New |
| Spa, shelf, features | Added within the shell's limits | Designed in |
| Permits and inspections | For the scope that counts as construction | Full permit |
| Disruption | Weeks, deck open | Months, yard open |

Our pool cost estimator prices a complete remodel below the same finished project built new, and the gap on a given pool is what you see when you run your own configuration. ## The usual answer

When the shell is sound and the location works, a complete remodel is the better decision for most homeowners: the pool you already paid for becomes the pool you want, the work takes weeks rather than months, and the budget goes into finish, equipment and features instead of excavation. New construction is the right call when the shell has failed, when the pool is in the wrong place, or when the whole backyard is being redesigned around a layout the current pool cannot fit.

What a remodel should not be is a surface fix on a pool with underlying problems. Resurfacing a leaking shell or re-tiling over a moving bond beam spends money twice. The difference between a surface job and a complete renovation is the subject of [resurfacing vs full renovation](/blogs/pool-resurfacing-vs-full-pool-renovation).

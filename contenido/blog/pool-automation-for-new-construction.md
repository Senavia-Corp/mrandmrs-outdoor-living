Automation is the part of a new pool that costs almost nothing to prepare for and a great deal to retrofit. The controller itself, the panel at the pad and the app on your phone, can be bought and installed at any point. What cannot be added without opening the deck is the conduit from the pad to the house, the actuators on the valves, the conductors for lights and features, and equipment chosen to be controlled rather than switched. This guide covers what automation does, what it needs buried, and how to decide what to install now and what to leave for later.

## What automation controls

A modern pool controller is a panel at the equipment pad with relays, valve actuator outputs and communication to the equipment, plus an app. On a residential pool it runs:

- **The pump**: speeds and schedules, slow for filtration most of the day, fast when a feature or the spa calls for it.
- **The heater** and its set points, including switching between pool and spa temperatures.
- **The valves** that send water to the pool, the spa, the sheer descents, the bubblers and the deck jets, with actuators doing what a person would otherwise do by hand at the pad.
- **The lights**: on, off, color and scenes for the pool, the spa and, if they are wired to it, the deck and the landscape.
- **Sanitation**: the output of a salt chlorine generator, and with sensors, pH and sanitizer monitoring and dosing.
- **Spa mode**: one button that closes the valves, raises the pump speed, starts the heater and turns on the jets.

## What has to be buried during construction

This is the whole reason to decide automation before the pad is set.

| Item | When it goes in | What it costs to add later |
|---|---|---|
| Conduit from the pad to the house or panel location | Rough plumbing and electrical | Trenching under the finished deck |
| Valve actuators on every pool/spa/feature valve | Equipment set | Re-plumbing the pad |
| Conductors for pool, spa and feature lights on separate circuits | Rough electrical | Pulling new wire through full conduit, or new conduit |
| Conduit for deck and landscape lighting | Before the deck is poured | Trenching |
| Automation-ready pump, heater and chlorinator | Equipment selection | Replacing equipment that works |
| Sensor ports for chemistry monitoring | Pad plumbing | Cutting into the plumbing |

Our guide to [upgrades worth planning before construction](/blogs/pool-upgrades-worth-planning-before-construction) sorts every upgrade on a proposal by the same rule: what closes at the shell, what needs something buried, and what can wait. Automation is the clearest example of the second category.

## Equipment that works with automation

The controller is only as useful as the equipment it can talk to.

- **Variable-speed pump.** Federal efficiency rules for dedicated-purpose pool pumps have meant that most new filter pumps are variable-speed since 2021, and that is what makes scheduling worthwhile: filtration at low speed for most of the day, full speed only when the spa or a feature needs it. The pump and the controller should speak the same protocol.
- **Heater or heat pump** with a control interface, so the set points live in the app. A heat pump that also cools matters on dark-finish pools and in the South Florida summer.
- **Salt chlorine generator** whose output the controller can set, so sanitation follows the pump schedule.
- **Lights** that accept color and scene commands from the controller rather than from a switch sequence.
- **Features** on their own valves with actuators, so each can be called separately.

We specify all of it as one system from one manufacturer family where that is possible, because mixed equipment is where automation becomes a service call. Which family depends on the project, and we go through the options in design.

## Deciding what to install now

The decision is three separate lists:

1. **Install during construction, no exceptions:** conduit, actuators, conductors, automation-ready equipment, sensor ports. This is the cheap part and it is the part that cannot wait.
2. **Install now if the budget allows:** the controller itself and the app. On most of our new pools it goes in with the equipment, because the spa, the heater and the features are easier to run from the first day.
3. **Add later without regret:** chemistry sensors and dosing, landscape lighting scenes, integration with a home system. All of it plugs into the prepared infrastructure.

If the controller is deferred, the pool is still fully usable; the owner runs the pad by hand and the pump on its own built-in schedule until the controller is added.

## Florida specifics

Three things change how we design automation here:

- **Lightning and power.** Surge protection at the pad and the panel is not optional in Florida, and the controller should recover its schedule after an outage without being reprogrammed.
- **Heat.** Scheduling the pump and the features at night moves water and sheds heat; a heat pump that cools becomes an automation set point rather than a manual job.
- **Weather and absence.** Many Florida owners are away for part of the year. Remote monitoring of pump status, water level, heater faults and chemistry is what keeps a pool from being a problem when nobody is home.
